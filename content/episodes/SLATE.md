# Launch slate — episodes 001–010

## Read this before using the slate

**The verdict is an output of research, not an input.**

Each brief below carries a *hypothesis* about which way the price moved. If
`trend-archaeologist` finds the evidence points the other way, the verdict changes — or the
episode dies. Writing the verdict first and then finding numbers to support it is exactly the
failure mode that turns a provenance channel into a rant channel, and it is how you eventually
publish something false.

**No prices appear in this file.** Every figure is marked `[VERIFY]` and must come from the
evidence pack with two independent sources. I have not pre-filled numbers from memory, because a
number I half-remember is indistinguishable from a number I invented once it reaches a script.
The verification checklist on each brief tells the researcher exactly where to look.

## 001 · `four-k-tv` — 🟢 STILL CHEAP
**Why it opens the channel.** The nostalgia wave is about 2016 feeling recent; a 4K TV is the
object that most viscerally proves it wasn't. Universally owned, universally shopped for, and
the delta is large enough to carry a hook without exaggeration.
**Excavation surprise:** panel oversupply and the shift to ad-subsidised smart TVs — you are not
paying for the panel any more, you are paying with attention.
**Verify:** Wayback snapshots of Best Buy / Amazon listings for a named 55" 4K model, Q4 2016 ·
current price from two live retailers, same day · BLS CPI series for televisions (this series is
one of the most dramatic in the entire CPI basket — confirm the exact ID) · state panel size and
resolution tier explicitly so the comparison is like-for-like.
**Affiliate:** yes — a current best-value 55–65" set.

## 002 · `fast-food-combo` — 🔻 THE DIG
**Why.** The single most felt price change of the decade and the most comment-generating topic
available. Everyone has a personal data point.
**Excavation surprise:** the gap between menu price and *app* price, and the shift to
loyalty-gated pricing — the menu board is no longer the real price.
**Verify:** archived menu boards or franchise price listings from 2016 (Wayback on the chain's
own site) · current national average, noting regional variation explicitly · BLS "food away from
home" series · name the exact item and size, since portion changes are the classic trap here.
**Affiliate:** no. Rage episode — placement here reads as cynical and converts at zero.

## 003 · `ssd-per-terabyte` — 🟢 STILL CHEAP
**Why.** The cleanest possible collapse and the easiest number to make dramatic on screen.
Strong purchase intent from a hobbyist audience that shares.
**Excavation surprise:** NAND layer-count scaling — the physical reason, explained in one line.
**Verify:** archived 2016 retail listings for a named 1TB consumer SSD · current price, two
retailers · normalize both to $/TB and say so on screen · hold the tier constant (consumer
SATA/NVMe — do not compare a 2016 SATA drive to a 2026 enterprise NVMe).
**Affiliate:** yes.

## 004 · `concert-fees` — 🔻 THE DIG
**Why.** High emotion, high shareability, and the fee structure is genuinely opaque — which is
exactly the kind of thing a receipt-based channel is built to expose.
**Excavation surprise:** the fee *share* of the total grew faster than face value. The story is
the ratio, not the price.
**Verify:** archived 2016 checkout screenshots or contemporaneous fee reporting · current
all-in checkout total for a comparable tier · compute fees as a % of total for both years —
that ratio is the episode.
**Editorial care:** mechanical causes only — exclusive ticketing agreements, dynamic pricing.
No named-individual blame (bible §7).
**Affiliate:** no.

## 005 · `streaming-stack` — ⚫ EXTINCT
**Why.** The purest expression of the nostalgia trend's actual emotional content: 2016 as the
last era of un-fragmented mass culture. This is the episode most likely to be shared with a
caption about "how it used to be."
**Excavation surprise:** the content didn't get more expensive — it got *divided*. Same library,
now behind N paywalls.
**Verify:** archived 2016 pricing pages for the major services · current prices for the set
needed to cover an equivalent library · be explicit that this is a constructed basket, not a
single product, and show the basket on screen.
**Affiliate:** no.

## 006 · `solar-per-watt` — 🟢 STILL CHEAP
**Why.** Genuinely useful, high-value purchase intent, and it lets the channel demonstrate it is
not just a rage feed. Good for brand safety and press citation.
**Excavation surprise:** manufacturing scale, not subsidy, did most of the work — a
counter-intuitive finding if the evidence supports it. **If it doesn't, report what it does.**
**Verify:** NREL or Lawrence Berkeley Lab installed-cost series (primary, authoritative, free) ·
module-only $/W vs installed $/W are *very* different numbers — pick one, label it clearly, and
do not let the script blur them. This is the highest risk of accidental misinformation in the
slate; flag it to `compliance-officer` explicitly.
**Affiliate:** cautiously — only if a genuinely good-value current product exists.

## 007 · `cinema-trip` — 🔻 THE DIG
**Why.** Pairs the price story with a nostalgia story about a vanishing ritual. Strong on both
axes the channel sits between.
**Excavation surprise:** the ticket roughly tracked inflation; concessions did not — and
concessions are where the economics of the entire industry now live.
**Verify:** NATO (National Association of Theatre Owners) average ticket price series, 2016 and
current · concession pricing is harder — archived menu boards or contemporaneous reporting, and
mark `estimated: true` if you cannot get two solid sources.
**Affiliate:** no.

## 008 · `genome-sequencing` — 🟢 STILL CHEAP
**Why.** The most extreme collapse available, and it makes the channel feel like it covers real
science rather than just retail. Differentiates hard from every other price channel.
**Excavation surprise:** the cost curve outran Moore's law for a decade — a genuinely
remarkable fact with authoritative free sourcing.
**Verify:** NHGRI "Cost of Sequencing" series (primary, free, authoritative — the ideal source) ·
distinguish *research* sequencing cost from *consumer test* price; they are different stories.
Pick one and hold it.
**Editorial care:** no medical claims of any kind (gate C8). This is about price, not health.
**Affiliate:** only for a consumer test, and only with the medical-claim line respected.

## 009 · `used-car` — 🔻 THE DIG
**Why.** Large, universal, and the one where the 2016→2026 line is genuinely strange rather than
just upward.
**Excavation surprise:** the shock never fully unwound — the price found a new floor rather than
returning to trend.
**Verify:** Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index (primary, free) · BLS used cars and trucks CPI ·
hold the segment constant (3-year-old midsize sedan) · **use the index, not anecdotes.**
**Affiliate:** no.

## 010 · `grocery-cart-001` — 🟡 TIME CAPSULE CART #001
**Why.** The series anchor and the primary subscribe driver, because it is explicitly serial and
numbered. Launch it in week one so the numbering starts early.
**Format note:** 8–12 named items, priced in both years, and the episode ends on the **total and
nothing else.** No commentary after the number. The number does the work — this is the one
episode where the writer's job is to shut up.
**Verify:** archived 2016 supermarket listings or BLS average price data (BLS publishes average
prices for specific items — eggs, bread, milk, ground beef — which is the strongest primary
source available for this episode) · current prices from one named retailer on one named day ·
show the full basket on screen with per-item citations.
**Affiliate:** no.

## Production order

Week 1: 001, 010, 002 — a collapse, the series anchor, and the highest-reach rage episode.
That mix establishes all three of the channel's modes before anyone decides what it is.

Then alternate STILL CHEAP and THE DIG strictly. Never publish two rage episodes back to back:
it reads as a grievance feed, and it starves the revenue segment.
