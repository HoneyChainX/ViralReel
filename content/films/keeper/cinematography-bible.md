# THE KEEPER — cinematography bible (previs-director)

One evening, one journey, one light. The film is about devotion as routine.

- **Lenses**: Act 1 (cottage/cliff) 35mm equiv — close, domestic. Act 2 (crossing)
  45mm — the sea flattens him small. Act 3 (arrival, 3D) 32→50mm push — the tower
  grows as duty nears.
- **Movement**: one idea per act: Act 1 lateral track (the walk), Act 2 slow push
  (the row), Act 3 rise + push (the climb of the light). No idle wobble — motion
  performs (HyperFrames doctrine, adopted).
- **Light**: the sun falls the whole film; Kip's lamp answers it at the end. Palette
  runs gold → slate → dusk-violet; Kip's mustard oilskin is the one constant warm
  note in every frame (canon phrase — never recolor).
- **Continuity**: Acts 1-2 are each ONE continuous shot (seamless chains, joins at
  motion valleys). Act cut 1→2 is a hard cut on action (door → oar). Act 2→3 earns
  a 0.5s dissolve (lane crossing = the film's one time-jump).
- **Grade**: colorist unifies with a gentle warm-dusk show LUT; the 3D act's AgX
  output is the grade anchor — 2.5D acts are pulled toward IT, never the reverse.
